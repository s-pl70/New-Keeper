# The Last Library

*Why I built the game*

I built The Last Library because I wanted to make something small and quiet in a semester that
often asked for big and loud. Most games are about doing — solving, fighting, winning. I wanted
to make a game about noticing. About the kind of attention that doesn’t get rewarded anywhere
else: the kind you give to a room, a book, a candle, a sleeping cat.


## About the Game

*Why this story* 

The story came from thinking about places that are disappearing — old libraries, quiet
neighborhoods, rooms nobody visits anymore. I kept returning to the idea that these places might
be waiting for someone, and that the simple act of paying attention could matter to them. The
library in the game is half-forgotten, but it is not dead. It just needs someone to listen to it before
the last candle goes out. I wanted to write something tender about that — about the people and
places we almost lose, and the small choice of staying with them a little longer.

## Built With

- **Twine** — for the narrative framing and branching choices at the beginning and end
- **Bitsy** — for the explorable library room, its sprites, and its dialogue
- **GitHub Pages** — for hosting

## What I wanted the players to feel

I wanted players to slow down. To feel a little lonely, a little curious, and then a little less lonely by
the end. I did not want them to feel clever for solving something — I wanted them to feel seen by
the game, the way the library notices them back. If a player finished the game and sat with it for a
moment before closing the tab, I would consider that a success.
